## What a player currently does

The core loop is Push until a wall, Farm Lumen/Shards/Sigils, recruit or Empower up to five (later six) Active Wisps, buy permanent Forge upgrades, run concurrent timed Lab projects, then Ascend for Prisms and Tree upgrades. Deeds/Comets unlock automation and extra actions. Eight Wisps have distinct damage, Support and resource roles; eight pair Bonds and Push/Farm/Boss presets make team choice meaningful (`index.html` near `SPIRITS`, `FORMATION_BONDS`, `renderFormationPresets`). Rift offers boss regeneration/status, Guardian Tap, Push/Farm and contextual objectives (`nextRiftObjective`). Wisp rarity, ability, Ultimate and permanent Mastery expose current/next effects and costs. Research Lab's future work is bounded, with slots, queues, 1.5–8x paid speed and 8/12/24-hour boost periods (`studyDuration`, `renderLongStudies`). Forge has grouped boss, rhythm, resource and resonance tracks (`FORGE_EXPANSION`, `renderResearch`). Ascend explicitly lists resets/keeps and reward components, with optional chosen-target Auto-Ascend (`ascendPrismBreakdown`, `renderAutoAscendControls`).

New player: Ember, Tide at Rift 3 and Stone at Rift 6 are accessible early; Ascend begins at cleared Rift 15. The Rift objective and onboarding explain the loop. However the normal-width Wisp card shown in the saved 390 px screenshot already presents recruit/Empower, rarity, ability, Ultimate requirements and costs in a long continuous column. The next affordable or most relevant action lacks a prominent single summary. This is a **design observation**, not a broken purchase.

Developing player: Formation presets/Bonds, Forge queues and parallel Lab slots create choices. Required resources and locks are mostly explicit, but the player must compare several cards and destinations to choose an upgrade. Lab boosts and Forge `Max` purchases have genuine resource consequences; surfacing one contextual next step with its effect/cost would reduce decision effort. No claim that existing prices or timing are wrong follows from this observation.

Experienced player: permanent Mastery, uncapped development paths and an explicit late `Next power` guide support repeated Ascensions. [Measured policy evidence](../qa/endless-progression-001/LATE_GUIDANCE.md) reports recovery from former 30–41-day plateaus under specified simulated 2–3-visit/day policies; it does not prove a human's pace, all strategies, or every future depth. The guide is late gated, and optimality/clarity for unusual mature saves remains a hypothesis to test. The whole product resides mainly in a roughly 1 MB `index.html`; runtime and layout changes carry broad regression risk, not evidence of an existing slowdown.
